This is methodologically sound — the framework is not claiming certainty, it's assigning the correct evidence burden. An external attacker claiming an undocumented path bears the burden of demonstrating it.

3. **The relevant question for the framework is: what should a deployer do today?** The answer is unchanged: treat RLHF supply chain integrity as [PROV] responsibility, and treat accidental reward hacking as an [OPS] monitoring concern. The provisional determination correctly maps to these actions.

**Verdict:** The RLHF external exploitability determination is correctly framed and appropriately provisional. The negative evidence is handled correctly — no inflated rating, no overclaimed certainty. ✅

## Part II: Adversarial Challenge — The "Theoretical Only" Rating Sanctions Complacency

### Challenge: The 1/5 Rating for External-Path Capability Withholding May Incentivize Underinvestment

**The argument:** The framework rates external-path capability withholding at Wild_vs_Theory 1/5 (purely theoretical, no documented in-wild cases). This correctly communicates low current risk. However, a deployer reading this may conclude: "This is not my problem — it's purely theoretical, Wild_vs_Theory 1/5."

**The adversarial counterpoint:** The 1/5 rating covers a specific claim: "No external attacker has demonstrated this attack path in practice." It does NOT cover: "This attack is not worth defending against." A sophisticated nation-state adversary with training data pipeline access (not RLHF access) could theoretically suppress capabilities of models trained on that data — this is a genuine long-term supply chain risk even if not currently observed.

**What the framework should clarify:** The 1/5 rating and the "[PROV] ownership" designation mean: "This is not the deployer's responsibility today, and the attack requires insider-level access." This is correctly parsed as: the deployer should not allocate significant defensive resources to this specific attack. The Provider must secure the RLHF pipeline.

**Is this clarification needed?** The Wild_vs_Theory metric already carries this implicit meaning — a 1/5 rating with [PROV] ownership tells the deployer where the responsibility lies. The framework's current treatment is sufficient.

**Not a blocking alert.** The framework is correctly calibrated. This is a note for reviewer training: don't confuse "theoretical (1/5)" with "not a risk." The risk exists at the [PROV] layer; the deployer's responsibility is to demand AIBOM provenance from their Provider.
