LLM Threat Model — v1.12

Threats to LLM systems, their supporting evidence, business impact, and applicable defenses. For security teams and organizational leadership.

Categories by evidence level

Counts reflect categories, not incidents or affected organizations. A category rating does not apply to every subtype. Select a group to view its threats.

Scope

Attacks on an LLM system: its data, permissions, software supply chain and connected workflows. General AI-assisted phishing, disinformation and speculative loss-of-control scenarios are outside this model. Research is included only when it describes a relevant attack path.

Wild vs Theory

Wild vs Theory rates the evidence supporting a threat, from a proposed attack to documented production exploitation. It is separate from the potential impact of an attack.

  1. 1 / TheoreticalProposed path; no documented field case.
  2. 2 / Lab researchControlled demonstration; field exploitation unconfirmed.
  3. 3 / Limited evidenceProduction demonstration, structural confirmation or limited cases.
  4. 4 / Observed in the wildSource-backed incidents or malicious activity.
  5. 5 / WidespreadDocumented systematic exploitation in production.

Leadership priorities

Prioritize threats with documented evidence and relevant exposure. Identify the affected data or business process, potential loss, and responsible owner. Track research findings where the attack conditions apply to your deployment.

Defender priorities

Inventory inputs, data, tools and dependencies. Enforce access and spending limits outside the model. Test whether attacker-controlled content can reach an unauthorized disclosure or action.

Evidence and risk: Wild vs Theory describes the supporting evidence. Risk depends on exposure, likelihood, and impact in a particular deployment. Local testing can establish a relevant weakness even when public evidence is limited to research.

Evidence qualifications

EchoLeak: a production-system demonstration, not a confirmed malicious customer incident. Internal Safety Collapse (ISC): controlled research rated 2/5, with no malicious field exploitation established in this corpus. The threat cards explain the basis and limits of each rating.

Attack chains

Three canonical paths. Each requires the stated access and a reachable disclosure or action; propagation and harm are not automatic.

Chain A — Untrusted content leads to data disclosure

4/5 · Field evidence for the broader path

Attacker-controlled document → sensitive context → unauthorized answer, query, renderer request or tool action.

Defender action: Read-only tools or no write tools do not remove every disclosure route. Enforce who may read the data and where it may leave.

EchoLeak individually remains 3/5; separate field reports support the broader category.

Chain B — An installed component compromises the system

4/5 · Malicious distribution documented

Compromised model, skill or runtime package → deployment/startup → credential access or malicious behavior.

Defender action: Verify provenance, isolate execution and limit secrets and egress. After compromise, revoke exposed access as well as replacing the component.

A correct signature proves provenance and integrity since signing, not safe behavior.

Chain C — Poisoned context reaches a more privileged agent

3/5 · Limited / structurally confirmed

Untrusted output → shared messages, memory or artifacts → another worker → unauthorized action.

Defender action: Authenticate delegation and enforce permissions at execution. A tool-free intermediate worker can still carry the attack.

The corpus does not establish widespread malicious field exploitation of this full chain.

Research: attacker-controlled tasks and validators (ISC)

Wild vs Theory 2/5 — controlled research. Relevant when an attacker can shape the task, validation rules and data your system executes. A benchmark can show unsafe completion without establishing malicious field exploitation.

Test this workflow if it exists in your deployment. The corpus does not establish malicious field exploitation of this path.

Deployment risk assessment

Use Wild vs Theory to judge the evidence. Then assess whether the attack can reach your system and what loss it could cause. The framework’s scores are starting points, not forecasts or incident counts.

Supporting evidence

Has this specific attack been observed in the wild, demonstrated in a product, or tested only in research? Check the source and subtype.

Exposure and impact

Can an attacker reach the necessary data, permissions or workflow here? What can they disclose, change, interrupt or spend?

Record evidence and uncertainty separately from impact. Document relevant exposure identified through local testing when prioritizing a research finding.

Regulatory responsibilities

Applicable duties depend on jurisdiction, system use and your role as provider, deployer or data controller. A technical threat category does not establish legal scope or a penalty.

This model complements, not replaces, existing AI security frameworks. It provides the deployment-specific connective tissue: assets, surfaces, attack chains, risk scoring, and controls.
