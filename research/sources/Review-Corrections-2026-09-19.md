# September 19 review: output handling, poisoning and legal scope

**Verified:** 2026-09-19. Primary references checked during the review; these are corrections to existing coverage, not new incident reports or effectiveness measurements.

- [OWASP LLM05:2025 Improper Output Handling](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm052025-improper-output-handling/) identifies browser XSS/CSRF, backend SSRF/code execution, SQL injection and path traversal from unchecked model output. It recommends treating output as untrusted, context-specific encoding, parameterized queries and browser content security policy. PI-DEF-4 operationalizes these at the actual receiving component, including streamed output; blocking tests and action-bound authorization are framework engineering requirements.
- [OWASP LLM04:2025 Data and Model Poisoning](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm042025-data-and-model-poisoning/) treats poisoning as an integrity attack, including falsified training documents and backdoors. Provenance, dataset versioning, vendor review, and comparison with trusted sources support DATA-DEF-1. The forged-policy RAG scenario is an engineering counterexample: a model can faithfully repeat false source data without following an injected instruction. It is not a claimed field incident.
- [EU AI Act, consolidated 2026-07-27 text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng): Article 6 and Annexes I/III govern high-risk classification; Articles 9-15 are high-risk system requirements. Article 16 addresses providers and Article 26 deployers of high-risk systems. Articles 53-54 address GPAI providers, with Article 55 additionally applying to systemic-risk GPAI providers. Article 50 transparency requirements are separately applicable. EU deployment or model nationality alone does not identify the entity's role or applicable obligations. Check applicable dates/exemptions in the current text; this corpus does not determine a real deployment's legal classification.
- [Aim Labs / Cato: EchoLeak](https://www.catonetworks.com/blog/breaking-down-echoleak/) describes employee-only access with attacker-supplied email, browser image-fetch disclosure, and no affected customers reported. Trusted users and absent model write tools do not exclude indirect disclosure. Retain the corpus's 3/5 demonstration rating for EchoLeak, separately from category-wide 4/5 evidence.

No new Wild_vs_Theory ratings or measured risk-reduction percentages are inferred from these corrections.
